# Step 1: Market Segmentation

## Starting point
Market pull, not tech push. Maria Chen spent 10 years running multi-unit restaurant operations
and is starting from a specific, repeatedly-observed problem (last-minute shift call-outs eating
manager time and causing late openings), not a technology looking for a home.

## Candidate segments
| # | Segment name | End user | Distinct need | Why it's heterogeneous from other segments | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multi-unit QSR franchise groups (10-50 units) | Shift manager/GM | Fast, ranked backup-worker text broadcast for hourly call-outs | Hourly shift labor, franchise-standardized ops, high call-out frequency | founder-identified |
| 2 | Multi-location casual-dining chains | Shift manager/GM | Same core need, but tipped-staff comp complicates the backup-worker incentive | Different comp structure changes who's willing to pick up a shift and why | founder-identified |
| 3 | Multi-location coffee/cafe chains | Shift lead | Same core need, smaller crews per shift | Smaller per-shift headcount means fewer backups needed but less redundancy | candidate — needs validation |
| 4 | Multi-location convenience-store retail | Store manager | Same core need, 24-hour coverage adds night-shift urgency | Overnight call-outs are a distinct, higher-stakes case (store can't just "run short") | candidate — needs validation |
| 5 | Hospital/healthcare per-diem nursing shift coverage | Charge nurse / staffing office | Fill a clinical shift with a *qualified/licensed* substitute, not just any warm body | Credentialing requirement makes the "qualified backup" filter far stricter | candidate — needs validation |
| 6 | Warehouse/logistics hourly shift workers | Shift supervisor | Fill a physical-labor shift fast to hit throughput SLAs | Different urgency driver (SLA penalty, not customer-facing lateness) | candidate — needs validation |
| 7 | Multi-location gyms/fitness studios | Front-desk lead | Fill a front-desk/instructor shift | Instructor certification adds a qualification filter similar to healthcare | candidate — needs validation |

| 13 | Single-location independent restaurants (large crew) | Owner/GM | Fill a call-out from a smaller, single-site labor pool | No cross-location backup pool possible — single point of failure | founder-identified |
| 14 | Movie theater chains | Location manager | Fill an usher/concession shift, event-driven demand spikes | Demand spikes are scheduled (release dates), not purely random like illness | candidate — needs validation |
| 15 | Multi-location childcare/daycare centers | Center director | Fill a state-mandated ratio-driven shift | Regulatory staffing ratios make a no-show a compliance issue, not just a service issue | candidate — needs validation |

## Segments carried forward to Step 2
1. **Multi-unit QSR franchise groups (10-50 units)** — Maria's direct network and domain expertise; highest reach and right-to-win.
2. **Multi-location casual-dining chains** — adjacent, similar shift structure, natural next pin if tipped-staff incentive design works.
3. **Multi-location convenience-store retail** — adjacent, 24-hour urgency is a distinct value-prop angle worth testing.
4. **Home health aide agencies** — structurally different (qualification-gated, off-site) but a large, underserved market if the product's "qualified backup" filter generalizes.
5. **School districts (substitute teacher coverage)** — large and analogous in shape, carried forward mainly as a runner-up to test against in Step 2 because of known entrenched incumbents.
