# Step 12: Determine the Customer's Decision-Making Unit (DMU)

Business-type branching used: **Consumer app** — the branch correctly predicted a collapsed DMU
(end user = economic buyer) and specifically named the two exceptions to check before collapsing
it: a household/social influencer, and a parental-veto case for minors. Both were checked directly
rather than assumed away.

## DMU roles

| Role | Who fills it (realistic title) | Same as End User? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| End User | Maya (or any beachhead-profile adult) | — | Uses the app daily |
| Champion | Same as End User | Yes | She decides to install and use it — no separate internal champion in a consumer self-serve context |

| Influencer/Recommender | **Real, distinct role — circle members and the friend who invited her** | No | Confirmed via the pilot and Step 9: every real prospect so far was reached through an existing relationship, and the circle-invite flow (Step 7) makes this role structurally load-bearing, not incidental |
| Veto-holder | **N/A for the target beachhead** | — | Checked the minor/parental-veto exception explicitly per this step's consumer-app branch: the beachhead profile (Step 3) is adults 24-42 who already own drawing tools and have independent purchasing power — no minors expected as a meaningful share of this specific beachhead. Flagged as a real exception to re-check if a future segment (e.g. teen sketchers) is ever pursued. |
| Procurement | N/A | — | Not applicable to a consumer self-serve purchase |

## Veto/blocker risks

None structural. The closest thing to a "veto" is the App Store's own review process (Apple can
reject an app or a specific feature) — this is a real gatekeeper, but it's a platform constraint on
the *product*, not a DMU role in the purchase decision itself; it's carried forward into Step 22's
MVBP scope instead.

## Typical deal size & complexity

One person, one decision, near-zero complexity — but with a real, structurally important
Influencer/Recommender role that the product spec deliberately builds around (the invite-after-first-
prompt flow), unlike a truly solo-use consumer product where that role wouldn't matter as much.

## Implications for the acquisition approach

Because the DMU collapses to one person but the Influencer/Recommender role is real and load-
bearing, the acquisition motion (Step 13) should lean on invite/referral mechanics, not just
top-of-funnel discovery — teed up for Step 13, not solved here.

## Assumptions flagged

None beyond what Step 3/9 already logged — this step's DMU mapping is a direct, confirmed
consequence of the beachhead profile, not a new hypothesis.
