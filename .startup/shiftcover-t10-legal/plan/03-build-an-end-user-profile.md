# Step 3: End User Profile

## Research basis
Of Maria's 14 informal conversations to date (logged during onboarding), only **3** were with the
actual end user this step profiles (shift managers/GMs) — the other 11 were with Directors of
Operations (peers from her own prior role, and the economic-buyer role, not the end user). This
is stated honestly rather than blended: **3 real conversations**, method = informal in-person/
phone conversation, not a structured interview script.

## Profile dimensions
| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| Firmographic/demographic anchor | General Manager or Shift Manager at one location within a 10-50 unit QSR franchise group. Non-exempt or low-tier-exempt, typically manages a crew of 15-40 hourly employees per location. |
| Behavioral characteristics | Already uses a scheduling tool (7shifts or HotSchedules, per franchise-group standardization) for the base schedule, but has no tool for same-day call-out coverage — falls back to a paper/laminated call list and a group text thread. No budget authority; software purchases are decided above them by the Director of Operations. Tech-comfortable in their personal life (uses consumer apps daily) but company-tool adoption is inconsistent because corporate mandates tools without asking GMs what they need. |
| Psychographic characteristics | Measured and stressed by two visible metrics: labor cost as % of sales, and on-time-opening compliance. Resents being blamed for a late opening caused by understaffing outside their control. Optimizes for "get through the shift without a visible failure," not long-term process improvement — there's no time in the day for the latter. |
| Distinguishing/trigger characteristic | Manages a location that is part of a multi-unit group with sister locations within reasonable driving distance (same metro) — this is what makes a cross-location backup pool plausible. A GM at a true single-location independent restaurant is *not* in this profile, even with an identical job title, because there is no sister-location backup pool to draw from. |

## Narrative profile
This person is a General Manager at one location of a 10-50 unit QSR franchise group who
currently handles same-day staffing call-outs by manually working a paper call list and a
company-wide group text thread, in rough seniority order, until someone answers — and struggles
with the fact that this process routinely takes 30-60 minutes during exactly the hours (early
morning, dinner rush) when they have the least slack to spend on it.

## In-profile vs. out-of-profile
**In profile:** GM/shift manager at a location that is one of several sister locations within the
same franchise group and metro area, using a standard scheduling tool already, with visible
labor-cost/on-time-open accountability.
**Out of profile (superficial lookalike):** a GM with an identical title at a true single-location
independent restaurant — same day-to-day pain, but no sister-location pool means the core product
mechanic (cross-location backup broadcast) doesn't apply; this is a different, harder problem
(recruiting external gig workers) that this beachhead does not attempt to solve.

## Relationship to the economic buyer
Different person. The GM is the end user; the Director of Operations (or Owner/Operator for
smaller groups) is the economic buyer and does not experience the day-to-day pain directly — full
DMU mapping deferred to Step 12.
