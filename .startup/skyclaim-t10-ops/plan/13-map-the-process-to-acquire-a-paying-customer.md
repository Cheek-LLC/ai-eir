# Step 13: Map the Process to Acquire a Paying Customer

Per this step's marketplace guidance: two parallel processes, never one blended map.

## DMU roles (from Step 12)
**Supply:** End User = Champion = Economic Buyer = the pilot. **Demand:** End User = ops manager,
Champion = ops manager (sometimes owner), Economic Buyer = owner/GM, Veto-holder = external
insurance carrier/adjuster.

## Acquisition process map — SUPPLY (pilot onboarding)
| Stage | DMU role(s) active | Trigger into this stage | What they need to believe | Objection | What kills the deal here | Unblocked by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Awareness | Pilot | Sees a post in the drone-operator Facebook group / meetup, or a direct outreach from Derek | "Is this legit and worth my time?" | "Another gig platform that races prices down" | A generic, unconvincing pitch that doesn't address the price-race concern directly | Derek's own peer credibility in the community |
| 2. Evaluation | Pilot | Reviews how jobs/pay are structured | "Will I actually get paid work, and on what terms?" | Unclear pay structure or exclusivity requirements | Vague answers about pay/terms | A concrete example job with real pay shown |
| 3. Sign-up & vetting | Pilot | Submits Part 107 cert, insurance proof, equipment info | "Is the vetting bar real (protects my reputation) or just paperwork?" | Vetting feels like busywork with no payoff if no jobs follow | Slow/opaque vetting with no job in sight | Fast turnaround, first job offered promptly after approval |
| 4. First job accepted & completed | Pilot | Sees and accepts a real nearby job | "Will this be worth it operationally?" | Job doesn't match location/pay expectations | A mismatched or underpaying first job | Accurate job details, fair first-job pay |
| 5. Repeat engagement | Pilot | Sees subsequent jobs over following weeks | "Is this a real, recurring income source?" | Job flow dries up after the first one | Thin, inconsistent job volume (Step 6's flagged renewal risk) | Real, sustained demand-side job volume |

## Acquisition process map — DEMAND (contractor onboarding)
| Stage | DMU role(s) active | Trigger into this stage | What they need to believe | Objection | What kills the deal here | Unblocked by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Awareness | Ops manager, owner | Derek's existing relationship, or trade-association reference | "Is this better than just calling Derek?" | "Why would I use an app instead of my existing guy?" | No clear reason to switch from the known-good status quo | Framing it as capacity-expansion, not a replacement for Derek |
| 2. Evaluation | Owner (economic buyer) | Reviews pricing and how the vendor relationship works | "Is this a real vendor I can rely on during a claim-window crunch?" | New-vendor risk during a time-critical event | Owner unwilling to risk an unproven vendor during peak season | A no-risk trial (first job at normal price, easy to revert to calling Derek if it fails) |
| 3. Carrier-acceptance check | Owner/ops manager, **external carrier (veto-holder)** | Owner asks internally (or informally checks with their carrier contacts) whether the report format will hold up | "Will my claims actually get approved with this documentation?" | Uncertainty whether an unfamiliar report format triggers carrier pushback | A carrier rejecting or questioning a SkyClaim-formatted report | A track record / reference from another contractor whose claims cleared using SkyClaim reports |
| 4. First booking | Ops manager | Posts first real job | "Will a pilot actually show up and deliver on time?" | Fear of a no-show during a time-critical window | An unfilled or late job | Reliable supply-side fulfillment (directly dependent on Step 4's thin-supply finding) |
| 5. Repeat/expanded use | Ops manager, owner | Books again in a subsequent storm event | "Is this now my default process?" | Reverting to old habits if the first experience was mediocre | A single bad experience during the highest-stakes moment (a live claim window) | Consistently good outcomes across multiple bookings |
