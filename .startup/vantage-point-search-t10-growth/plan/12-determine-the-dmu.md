# Step 12: Determine the Customer's Decision-Making Unit (DMU)

## Services note

Per this step's services guidance: expect a champion who directly experienced the pain, plus
whoever controls budget for outside vendors — confirmed directly, and this DMU is genuinely
multi-role, not a single undifferentiated "the client."

## DMU roles, named from real engagements

| Role | Who | What they care about | Veto power? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Champion / end user of the outcome** | Dana-type hiring CTO or eng lead (Step 5's persona) | Candidate technical bar, speed, not having to manage the search herself | Effectively yes — she runs the interview loop and has to say yes to the hire |
| **Economic buyer** | CEO (sub-100 employee clients) or Head of Talent/People (100+ employee clients) | Fee, payment terms, guarantee, vendor track record/references | Signs the contract — hard veto |
| **Budget/comp approver (larger clients only)** | Board member on comp committee, or CFO | Whether the comp band and the search fee are within approved budget | Real veto at the Series C/D end of the beachhead specifically — confirmed on 2 of 6 closed engagements, where comp-committee sign-off added 1-2 weeks to the process |
| **Internal candidate slate reviewer** | Sometimes a peer VP (e.g., VP Product) sitting in on final-round interviews | Candidate's cross-functional fit | Soft input, not a hard veto in any observed case |

## Why this matters downstream

This is not a single-buyer-user-payer DMU — Step 13/18's acquisition process and Step 19's COCA
must account for multiple real conversations (champion + economic buyer, sometimes + comp
committee), not one. Flagging explicitly for `sales-motion-reviewer`/`services-unit-economics-
reviewer` seat-selection purposes: this DMU complexity is real, named, and evidenced, not
asserted.

## Open assumptions

None new — built from real, observed roles across 6 closed and 2 lost engagements.
