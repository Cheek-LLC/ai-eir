# Step 2: Select a Beachhead Market

## Beachhead selected

**Segment 1 — VP/Director of Engineering search for VC-backed Series B-D startups (50-500
employees).**

## Rationale against Aulet's actual criteria (not just "biggest segment")

- **Reachable with current resources.** Jordan spent 11 years as an in-house technical recruiter
  at three VC-backed startups in exactly this stage band before going independent — his existing
  network (candidates, hiring managers, VC talent partners) is concentrated here, not a network he
  has to build from zero.
- **Currently underserved.** Two structural gaps in this segment: (1) big retained-search firms
  (Heidrick & Struggles, Spencer Stuart, Riviera Partners) generally decline searches below
  ~$150K in fees, which rules out most Series B-D VP/Director-level roles; (2) generalist
  contingency recruiters lack the technical fluency to credibly screen a VP Engineering candidate
  on architecture/scaling judgment, so hiring managers don't trust them with a senior technical
  search. Jordan sits in the gap.
- **Real evidence, not size alone.** 6 of Jordan's 8 signed engagements in 14 months came from
  this exact segment — this beachhead already has real signed revenue behind it, not just a
  hypothesis (see Step 9).
- **Referenceable into adjacent segments.** A successful VP Eng placement at one VC-backed
  portfolio company is a real reference other partners at the *same VC firm* ask about for their
  other portfolio companies — this is Jordan's actual bowling-pin mechanism (VC-firm-level trust,
  not just company-level), and it's already happened once (see Step 9's referral-source detail).

## Segments explicitly ruled out for now

- Segment 5 (growth-stage C-level) — real fee upside, but Jordan would be competing directly
  against firms with brand and bench depth he doesn't have; revisit only after the beachhead
  proves the model and funds building that credibility.
- Segment 6 (contingency eng-manager/IC) — a volume, race-to-fill market that would compete
  directly for the same limited hours Segment 1 needs, at a much lower fee per hour of delivery
  effort; explicitly rejected as a distraction from the capacity-constrained core business, not
  as a bad market in the abstract.

## Open assumptions

None new here — this step reasons from Step 1's segmentation and Jordan's own operating history,
not an unvalidated projection.
