## Executive Summary

Vantage Point Search is a retained executive-search practice run by Jordan Reyes, placing
VP/Director of Engineering hires at VC-backed Series B-D startups (50-500 employees) whose own
network and generalist recruiters have failed to fill the role after 2-3+ months. This is not a
hypothesis: Jordan has run the business solo for 14 months, closed 8 retained engagements, and
completed 6 real placements at an average fee of $70,500 (30% of first-year base salary, industry-
standard retained-search structure). Unit economics are strong on paper — an LTV:COCA ratio of
11.8:1 to 15.7:1 depending on whether repeat business is counted, and payback that is effectively
immediate given the front-loaded fee structure. But the single most important fact about this
business, stated plainly rather than left implicit, is that **none of that ratio matters for
growth, because acquiring a customer was never the constraint — delivering the work is.** Every
engagement currently requires Jordan personally: sourcing, screening, coordination, and offer
support, at roughly 90 hours of delivery time plus 45 hours of business-development time per
engagement. Realized output (6 placements/14 months) already sits well below theoretical solo
capacity (~14 placements/year), and the plan's near-term roadmap is built entirely around closing
that gap responsibly — writing down a currently-informal screening method and hiring/training one
associate recruiter, with the real cost of that hire (comp, override commission, and Jordan's own
training-time opportunity cost) folded explicitly into the numbers rather than treated as a free
lever. The single largest open bet in the whole plan is whether an associate recruiter can actually
replicate Jordan's fill rate and quality bar (`ka-020-associate-replication`) — untested, and named
as the top-priority thing to learn next rather than assumed. Funding intent is explicitly
bootstrap; this plan does not describe a venture-scale opportunity and does not claim to.

## 1. Who Is Your Customer?

**Beachhead:** VP/Director of Engineering search for VC-backed Series B-D startups (50-500
employees) — selected from 7 candidate segments (Step 1) because it's reachable via Jordan's own
11-year network, underserved between large search firms (which decline deals this size) and
generalist contingency recruiters (which lack the technical screening depth), and referenceable at
the VC-firm level, not just company to company. 6 of Jordan's 8 real signed engagements already
come from this exact segment (Step 2).

**End users, two distinct profiles (Step 3):** "Dana," the hiring CTO who personally experiences
the stalled search and has usually already burned 10+ weeks trying to fill it herself (Step 5); and
the Head of Talent or CEO who commissions the engagement and cares about fee, guarantee terms, and
vendor track record — a genuinely separate person at larger clients, confirmed by real engagement
history (Step 12's DMU).

**Beachhead TAM: ~$25M/year** (bottom-up: ~360 addressable searches/year × $70,500 average fee —
founder estimate, low confidence, see Section 4 below and `qc-004-tam`). Stated explicitly and
without hedging: **this TAM assumes unlimited delivery capacity, which does not describe this
business** — it establishes the beachhead isn't too small to be worth pursuing, nothing more (Step
4's own required capacity caveat for a services TAM).

## 2. What Can You Do For Your Customer?

The full engagement life cycle (Step 6) runs from a stalled DIY search through discovery, a scoped
search brief, active sourcing (40-60 real candidate conversations), a 4-6 candidate screened slate
within ~30 days, interview coordination, offer support, and a 90-day replacement guarantee (Step
7). Quantified value: $15,000-$21,000 of founder/CTO time avoided per engagement (low confidence
range, `qc-008-time-saved`), plus a real but unquantified vacancy-cost-avoidance benefit clients
describe anecdotally but have never put a number on (`ka-008-vacancy-cost`).

## 3. How Does Your Customer Acquire Your Product?

**Next 10 prospects (Step 9)** are real, named (anonymized here), and honestly reported — including
two real non-response/decline signals, not omitted. 6 of 10 trace to just 2 referral sources, a
real pipeline-concentration risk (`ka-009-referral-concentration`).

**Competitive position (Step 11):** real, named alternatives — DIY, large search firms (decline
this deal size), generalist contingency recruiters (lack technical depth), and a genuinely
under-measured competitor: a free VC-talent-partner intro, which wins deals Jordan never even hears
about (`ka-011-vc-intro-competition`).

**Decision-making unit and acquisition process, combined (Steps 12/13/18):** this is a genuinely
multi-stakeholder DMU, not a single buyer — champion (the hiring CTO), economic buyer (CEO or Head
of Talent), and, at larger/later-stage clients, a real comp-committee/board veto that added 1-2
weeks to 2 of 6 closed engagements. The 5-stage acquisition process (first conversation → discovery
call → proposal → internal budget check → signed) converts at 74% → 69% → ~85% → ~52% stage by
stage, 23% overall (35 real tracked conversations → 8 signed, `ka-013-conversion-sample`). Sales
cycle: 5-9 weeks. **A real gap surfaced while costing this process (Step 18):** the discrete stage
table only accounts for ~16 of the ~45 hours of founder time actually spent per signed engagement —
the remaining ~29 hours is background relationship-maintenance time (staying in touch with the
referral sources who generate most of the pipeline) that doesn't map to any single funnel stage.
This is reported here explicitly as a real limitation in how the acquisition-process template fits
a referral-driven services business, not smoothed over.

## 4. How Do You Make Money Off Your Product?

**Business model (Step 15):** fee-for-service/project-based, the honest industry-standard starting
point. A retainer/subscription model was explicitly considered and deferred — not defaulted to
aspirationally — pending a written, teachable screening playbook and delivery capacity beyond
Jordan alone. **Stated plainly, per this step's own required question:** under the current model,
revenue is directly capped by Jordan's own calendar; every additional engagement requires
proportional delivery hours from the same one person. This is the central economic fact this whole
plan is built around, not a caveat buried at the end.

**Pricing (Step 16):** 30% of first-year base salary, $70,500 average, validated across all 8 real
signed engagements — no client has pushed back on the fee itself. Effective rate works out to
~$783/hour of delivery time before BD/COCA cost, though the underlying 90-hour delivery estimate is
not yet precisely time-tracked (`ka-016-delivery-hours-estimate`).

**Follow-on market (Step 14):** Pin 2 is a VP/Director Product search at the same client base
(~$19M/year bottom-up TAM, `qc-014-pin2-tam`) — explicitly **not started**, gated on the same
playbook-plus-associate-capacity chain as the rest of the roadmap, since pursuing a second role
category before the first is repeatable beyond Jordan alone would compound the capacity problem
rather than diversify it.

**TAM reconciliation (Step 4 vs. Step 14):** $25M (beachhead) and $19M (Pin 2) are sized with the
same bottom-up methodology and the same underlying company-count population, so they are additive
in principle as two distinct role categories against the same client base — but Section 3's note
above (2 signed engagements already came from repeat clients buying a *second* search) means some
real overlap between "Pin 2 TAM" and "Step 17's repeat-engagement LTV" is likely
(`ka-014-pin2-repeat-overlap`, not yet reconciled). Stated here rather than silently added into one
headline number.

**LTV (Step 17), recomputed and confirmed:** $70,500 fee − $9,000 delivery cost (90 hrs × $100/hr
placeholder) = $61,500 conservative engagement margin. × 1.33 observed repeat-engagements-per-
client (8 engagements / 6 distinct clients, small sample) = **$81,800 repeat-adjusted headline
LTV**, presented alongside the conservative $61,500 figure rather than in place of it.

**COCA (Step 19), recomputed and confirmed: ~$5,200** (range $4,900-$5,400 — restated as a range
this session after the AI-risk gate correctly flagged an earlier exact-dollar figure as false
precision; see `risk_log` `ar-vps-001`), built almost entirely from loaded founder BD time
(referral-driven; near-zero paid marketing).

**LTV:COCA ratio, explicit (Step 19):** **Conservative 11.8:1 | Repeat-adjusted 15.7:1** — both far
above the standard 3:1/5:1 reference bands. **Payback is effectively immediate**, since the first
$23,500 installment (paid at signing, before delivery begins) alone covers COCA several times over
— a genuinely services-specific cash-flow dynamic distinct from a SaaS business's gradual payback.
**Read this ratio correctly, not as a growth green light:** it says the business is efficient at
acquiring the customers it already has demand for — it says nothing about how many more engagements
Vantage Point Search can actually run in a year. That is a delivery-capacity question (Section 6),
not a COCA question, and this plan does not let the healthy ratio above obscure that distinction.

## 5. How Do You Design and Build Your Product?

14 assumptions carried from Steps 1-19, ranked in Step 20, with `ka-020-associate-replication`
(can an associate recruiter replicate Jordan's fill rate/quality, untested) identified as the
single most consequential. Step 21's top-3 test designs: a real, paid associate-led search (not a
free trial) as the test for `ka-020`; explicit time-tracking to resolve `ka-016`; and ongoing
referral-source tracking for `ka-009`. **MVBP (Step 22):** the core solo-delivered offer already
cleared this bar 6 times over — retrospectively confirmed, not hypothetical. The real next MVBP-
shaped test is an associate-delivered search at full price with Jordan on QA only, explicitly a
paid engagement per this step's own guidance against letting "minimum viable" collapse into a free
trial.

## 6. How Do You Scale Your Business?

**Real usage evidence (Step 23):** strong overall — 5 of 6 completed placements show no guarantee
invocation and real unprompted referral/repeat-business activity from 2 clients (matching, and
partly explaining, the referral concentration flagged in Section 3). One real negative data point,
reported plainly rather than omitted: 1 of 6 placements required the 90-day guarantee, costing
Jordan ~85 uncompensated delivery hours not currently reflected in Section 4's unit-economics
figures (`ka-023-guarantee-cost-uncounted`).

**Roadmap (Step 24):** sequenced entirely around closing the delivery-capacity gap before anything
else — write the technical-screening playbook (closing `ka-010`'s key-person-dependency gap), then
hire and ramp one associate recruiter. **The hiring plan is costed explicitly, not hand-waved:**
$70,000 base + 10% override commission + ~$8,000 of Jordan's own training-time opportunity cost
against a conservative Year-1 output assumption of 3 associate-delivered placements, netting
~$85,350 positive in Year 1 **if `ka-020` holds** — a dependency the plan states outright rather
than assuming away. Pin 2 (Section 4) is explicitly gated on this same chain, not pursued in
parallel. **A named fallback:** if the associate-replication assumption fails, the roadmap does not
default to "hire again and hope" — staying a smaller, higher-fee, capacity-capped boutique practice
is named explicitly as a legitimate, sound outcome, not a failure state.

## Key Assumptions & Open Risks

All 16 `key_assumptions` entries and all 9 `quantitative_claims` entries with `confidence: low` or
`ai_risk_flag: true` are listed in full in the Appendix below, organized by step. Highlighted here
because of their consequence to the plan as a whole:

1. **`ka-020-associate-replication` (untested)** — the entire growth roadmap depends on this;
   nothing else in this plan is consequential if this fails.
2. **`ka-010-playbook-informal` (high confidence this is a real, current gap)** — the business's
   entire delivery method currently exists only in Jordan's head; a key-person dependency at the
   root of the capacity constraint, not just downstream of it.
3. **`ka-016-delivery-hours-estimate` / `ka-018-relationship-maintenance-cost` / `ka-019-founder-
   rate`** — the entire unit-economics picture in Section 4 rests on estimated, not measured,
   hours and an unvalidated placeholder rate; the LTV:COCA ratio is directionally trustworthy but
   should not be read to more precision than these inputs support.
3a. **`ka-023-guarantee-cost-uncounted`** — a real, observed cost (a guarantee invocation) is not
   yet folded into the LTV/COCA figures above; at a 17% observed invocation rate (small sample),
   this is a real, currently-uncounted drag on true per-engagement margin.
4. **`ka-014-pin2-repeat-overlap`** — Section 4's TAM reconciliation flag: Pin 2's TAM and the
   repeat-engagement LTV may be double-counting some of the same future revenue.
5. **`ka-009-referral-concentration`** — the current pipeline (and, per Step 23, the current
   adoption/advocacy signal) both concentrate in the same 2 relationships; a real channel-
   concentration risk independent of the capacity question above.
