## 1. Positioning statement

> For **VP/Director-of-Engineering hiring managers at VC-backed Series B–D startups (50–500
> employees)** — technical co-founders and CTOs like "Dana," who have already spent 10+ weeks
> trying to fill the role through their own network and gotten nothing that clears their bar —
> **Vantage Point Search** is a **retained executive-search practice built specifically for
> technical leadership hires** that **delivers a screened, 4–6 candidate slate you can actually
> run through your own interview loop within about 30 days, sourced from real conversations with
> people who've already run engineering orgs through a scaling inflection point** (`qc-016-price`,
> Step 7 delivery spec). Unlike **doing the search yourself** — the thing every one of Jordan's six
> real clients tried first — Vantage Point Search **replaces 100+ hours of your own sourcing time
> with a fee that's a fraction of the leadership-vacancy cost, without the technical-screening gap
> generalist recruiters bring to a role like this.**

**Why this alternative, not a company-shaped competitor:** Step 11 names DIY as the real baseline
every actual client tried first — not a hypothetical "vs. Recruiter X" framing. Large search firms
(Heidrick & Struggles, Spencer Stuart, Riviera Partners) decline this deal size outright; generalist
contingency recruiters compete on price and volume, not technical depth. The positioning leads with
DIY because that's the actual switching decision a prospect is making, and names the other two as
secondary contrasts in the objection-handling section below, not in the headline statement.

**Why this persona, not "startups" broadly:** Step 2's beachhead is reachable (Jordan's own 11-year
network sits here), underserved (the gap between big firms and contingency recruiters), and already
proven (6 of 8 real signed engagements). Positioning to "startups" or "tech companies" broadly would
be positioning past the evidence base — every proof point below only holds for this specific band.

## 2. Messaging pillars

**Pillar 1 — Technical screening depth a generalist can't match.**
Claim: "Every candidate on your slate has been screened on the specific architectural and
scaling-judgment questions your org actually needs answered — not just resume keywords."
Proof: Jordan's 11 years as an in-house technical recruiter at three VC-backed startups in this
exact stage band (`business_basics.business_type_notes`); Step 11's competitive gap — generalist
recruiters "lack the technical fluency to credibly screen a VP Engineering candidate on
architecture/scaling judgment," named explicitly by multiple past clients as why they moved on.
Why Dana cares: her own account of the problem is not "I can't evaluate candidates," it's "I don't
have time to go find ten of them to compare" — this pillar speaks to exactly that gap, not a
generic "we find great people" claim.

**Pillar 2 — Real time back, not just a filled seat.**
Claim: "Get out from under 10+ hours a week of sourcing you don't have room for, without lowering
the bar."
Proof: `qc-008-time-saved` — $15,000–$21,000 of founder/CTO time avoided per engagement (explicitly
a range, `confidence: low` — a founder estimate from client discovery-call accounts, not
independently verified against any client's actual comp; stated honestly, not oversold as a precise
figure).
Why Dana cares: her stated goal, verbatim from Step 5, is "get her own time back — every week this
stays open is a week she's doing two jobs."

**Pillar 3 — A real, proven track record, not a pitch.**
Claim: "6 real placements in 14 months, zero guarantee invocations lost to candidate quality, and
clients who've come back for a second search."
Proof: Step 23's dogfood evidence — 5 of 6 completed placements show no guarantee invocation, and 2
clients have generated real unprompted referrals or repeat business. Stated with the honest
counterpoint too (see objection-handling #5 below) — this pillar does not hide the one guarantee
invocation that did happen.
Why Dana (and the economic buyer) care: a services purchase this expensive is trust-driven first;
this is the credibility pillar the Geoffrey Moore template's "why believe this" gap needs filled.

**Pillar 4 — A defined, bounded process with a real guarantee, not an open-ended retainer.**
Claim: "One scoped engagement, a 4–6 candidate slate in about 30 days, and a 90-day replacement
guarantee if the hire doesn't work out — not an open-ended monthly retainer you have to manage."
Proof: Step 7's product spec and Step 15's business-model decision (project-based, not retainer —
explicitly chosen over the recurring-revenue-friendlier retainer model because it's the honest fit
for what clients are actually buying right now).
Why the economic buyer (CEO / Head of Talent) cares: per Step 12's DMU, this role cares about fee,
terms, and vendor track record — a bounded scope with a real guarantee is a lower-risk commitment
than an open retainer with an unclear end date.

## 3. Elevator pitches

**10-second:** "I run technical-leadership searches for VC-backed startups whose own network just
ran out — VP/Director of Engineering, specifically."

**30-second:** "I'm Jordan Reyes, I run Vantage Point Search. I place VP and Director of
Engineering hires at Series B through D venture-backed startups — specifically for founders and
CTOs who've already spent two or three months trying to fill the role themselves and hit a wall.
I spent 11 years as an in-house technical recruiter before going independent, so I actually know
how to screen for architectural and scaling judgment, not just keywords. Six placements in the
last 14 months, all still in seat."

**2-minute:** "I'm Jordan Reyes, I run Vantage Point Search — a retained search practice built for
one specific, painful moment: a Series B–D startup's VP or Director of Engineering role has been
open for two-plus months, the founder or CTO has burned through their own network trying to fill
it, and the candidates coming through generalist recruiters aren't clearing the technical bar. I
spent 11 years as an in-house technical recruiter at three VC-backed startups before going
independent 14 months ago, so I'm not learning how to screen for this on the client's dime — it's
what I actually did for a decade before I started doing it as a search practice. In that time I've
run 8 retained engagements and closed 6 real placements, at an average fee of $70,500 — 30% of
first-year base, industry-standard structure, paid in three installments so you're never fronting
the whole fee before you've seen a slate. You get a scoped search, 40 to 60 real candidate
conversations, a screened slate of 4 to 6 people within about 30 days, and a 90-day replacement
guarantee if it doesn't work out. I'm not the right fit for every search — if you need a
C-level hire or a role outside engineering leadership at this stage, that's a different
conversation — but if the problem is specifically 'I need someone who's run an engineering org
through a scaling inflection point, and I don't have three more months to find them myself,' that's
exactly what I do, and I can walk you through exactly how the last six searches went."

## 4. Tagline options

1. **"Technical searches, run by someone who's actually run technical orgs."** — Leads with the
   credibility differentiator (Pillar 1); tradeoff: doesn't lead with speed/time, which is Dana's
   more emotionally immediate pain.
2. **"Your VP Eng search, off your plate in 30 days."** — Leads with the time/speed outcome
   (Pillar 2); tradeoff: the "30 days" figure is a real operational figure from Step 7 but doesn't
   carry its own `quantitative_claims` sourcing entry the way the dollar figures do — use with the
   same "about" hedge the plan itself uses, not as a guarantee.
3. **"Six searches. Six placements. Zero surprises."** — Leads with the track-record pillar;
   tradeoff: doesn't mention the one real guarantee invocation, which is why this line is only
   used alongside, never instead of, honest disclosure if a prospect asks about guarantee history.
4. **"The technical screen your last recruiter couldn't run."** — Leads with the competitive
   differentiation vs. generalist recruiters (Step 11); tradeoff: implicitly assumes the prospect
   has already tried a generalist recruiter, which per Step 9 is common but not universal among the
   next-10 list.

**Recommended: #2** — "Your VP Eng search, off your plate in 30 days." Dana's own stated goal
(Step 5) is getting her time back more than anything else; the number is concrete and immediately
legible in a way "technical depth" as a headline is not. Pair it with Pillar 1 as the first
follow-up line in any longer copy, since depth is what makes the 30-day number credible rather than
suspicious.

## 5. Objection handling

1. **"Why not just keep trying to fill it ourselves — we're close."** (Dana, most common real
   objection per Step 9's DIY baseline) — Genuine rebuttal: "You've already spent 10+ weeks on this.
   The real question isn't whether you *can* eventually fill it yourself — it's whether the next
   4-8 weeks of your own sourcing time is worth more or less than the fee. For most of my clients,
   at their stage, it's not close." Not a deflection — this is literally Step 8's value-prop math,
   stated honestly with its range and low-confidence flag intact.

2. **"Your fee is a lot more than a contingency recruiter's 10-15%."** (economic buyer) — Genuine
   rebuttal: "It is. A contingency recruiter's fee is lower because their business model is volume —
   they're not built to screen for VP-Engineering-level architectural judgment, and several of my
   current clients tried that route first and moved on because of it (Step 11). You're not paying
   more for the same screen, you're paying for a screen a contingency model structurally can't do at
   that price." Honest, not oversold — doesn't claim contingency recruiters are bad at everything,
   just names the specific gap.

3. **"How do we know this isn't just luck — six placements is a small sample."** (financially
   literate economic buyer / board) — Honest answer, not a rebuttal that oversells: "You're right
   that it's a real but limited sample — 8 signed engagements, 6 completed placements, over 14
   months. I'd rather you know that going in than imply more statistical weight than it has. What I
   can point to is the process itself: 40-60 real candidate conversations per search, and a track
   record you can check with the two clients who've come back for a second search." This objection
   doesn't have a stronger answer than that yet — flagged honestly per this skill's own instruction,
   not spun.

4. **"What if the person you place doesn't work out?"** — Genuine rebuttal: "90-day replacement
   guarantee — if the hire leaves inside 90 days, I re-run the search at no additional fee. That's
   happened once, out of six placements, and I honored it in full." States the real 1-of-6 rate
   (`qc-023-guarantee-invocation-rate`) rather than implying a perfect record.

5. **"We're also talking to [name of a VC talent partner about a free intro]."** — Honest answer:
   "That's a real, valid option, and it works sometimes — I'd rather you take it if it gets you the
   right hire faster and for free. What I'd ask is: has that conversation actually produced a
   candidate who clears your bar yet, or is it still open the way the rest of your search has been?
   If it closes this for you, genuinely, that's a good outcome — I'm not going to pretend a free
   in-network intro isn't real competition." This is the honest answer to Step 11's named
   under-measured competitor — no rebuttal that pretends this alternative doesn't work.

## 6. Voice and tone guardrails

**Banned:** revolutionary, game-changing, best-in-class, seamless, unlock, empower, disrupt,
synergy, world-class, rockstar/ninja/guru (candidate-market language Dana would find alienating
given her own frustration with generalist recruiters' pattern-matching).

**Register to write in:** direct, numbers-first, low-hype — closer to how Jordan describes the
business to a peer founder than how a recruiting agency markets itself. Dana is a technical
operator who has already been disappointed by recruiter marketing language once (the generalist
recruiters Step 11 names); copy that sounds like typical staffing-agency marketing actively works
against this pillar's credibility, not just neutrally.
