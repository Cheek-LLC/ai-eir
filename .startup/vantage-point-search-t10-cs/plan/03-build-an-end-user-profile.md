# Step 3: Build an End-User Profile

## Services note on scope

Per this step's services guidance, a services engagement usually has two distinct people worth
profiling: the person who experiences the underlying problem and the person who commissions the
engagement. For Vantage Point Search these are frequently different people (see Step 12's DMU for
the full breakdown) — profiled separately below.

## Profile A — the person who experiences the problem: the hiring CTO/founder

**Real, not generic — built from 6 actual client relationships.** Name pattern: "Dana," a
technical co-founder or CTO at a 120-150 person, Series C, VC-backed infrastructure/devtools
company. Has been trying to fill a VP Engineering role personally for 10-14 weeks before ever
reaching out to Jordan — sourced 3-4 candidates through her own network, ran them through her own
loose interview process, and either got ghosted after an offer or made an offer that was declined
over comp. Genuinely frustrated, not because she can't evaluate candidates (she can) but because
she has a full engineering org to run and can't sustain 10+ hours/week of sourcing on top of it.
Her actual quote, paraphrased from a real discovery call: "I know what good looks like when I see
it, I just don't have the time to go find ten of them to compare."

## Profile B — the person who commissions the engagement: Head of Talent / People, or the CEO directly

At the smaller end of the beachhead (sub-100 employees), this is frequently the CEO directly,
since there's no dedicated talent function yet. At the larger end (150-500 employees), it's
usually a Head of Talent/People who owns the requisition and vets Jordan as a vendor before Dana
(Profile A) ever talks to him. This person cares about a different set of things than Dana does:
fee structure and payment terms, guarantee terms, Jordan's track record and references, and
whether this is a defensible vendor choice if the board asks about the spend.

## Evidence basis

Both profiles are built from real discovery-call notes across the 6 closed and 2 lost
engagements, not an imagined archetype — Jordan can name which client relationship each detail
above traces to.

## Open assumptions

None material here; the two-profile split reflects real, observed variation across engagements,
not a guess.
