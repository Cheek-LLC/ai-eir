# Step 5: Persona for the Beachhead Market

Per this step's marketplace branching guidance: build the persona from whichever side is the
harder constraint to win first — Step 4 established that's **supply** (pilots) for SkyClaim, so
the primary persona below represents that side. A demand-side persona is sketched second (not
required by this step to call it done, but drafted anyway since Derek's demand-side relationships
are actually his strongest asset and skipping a persona there would understate a real strength).

## Persona snapshot (primary — supply side)
**Marcus Webb**, 34, Fort Worth, TX. Runs a small real-estate/event photography business
(Webb Aerial Media) as his primary income, Part 107-certified for 4 years, owns a DJI Mavic 3
Enterprise. One of Derek's actual named contacts (real individual, not composited).

## Day in the life
Marcus's weekdays are mostly real-estate listing shoots booked through his own client base —
steady but modest income, heavily dependent on the local housing market's pace. Weekends are
open more often than he'd like. He first flew a storm-inspection job informally for Derek two
years ago (as an Osei Aerial subcontractor on an overflow day) and liked it — higher per-flight
pay than a real-estate shoot, and a different kind of flying challenge. The problem shows up
exactly there: he has no reliable channel to *find* more of that work. He's not going to cold-call
roofing contractors himself — that's not his business, and he doesn't have the insurance-industry
relationships to know who to call.

## Goals & how they're measured
Grow monthly income without giving up the flexibility that made him leave a W-2 job in the first
place. He doesn't track a formal KPI, but he mentally tallies "good months" as ones where paid
flight hours cross roughly 15 hours outside his core real-estate work.

## Frustrations & current workaround
Current workaround is entirely informal — word of mouth, the Facebook group, and whichever
solo operators (like Derek) happen to think of him when they're overbooked. He's tried a
general-purpose gig drone platform once and found it "a race to the bottom on price, mostly
real-estate shoots I already do myself, nothing storm-specific."

## Decision-making style & trusted sources
Trusts peer recommendation over marketing — he joined the DFW drone-operator Facebook group
specifically because another pilot vouched for it. Skeptical of anything that reads as a
"platform trying to own the relationship" rather than a tool that gets him paid work.

## Representative quote
"I don't need someone managing my calendar. I need someone putting real, well-paying jobs in
front of me that I can say yes or no to on my own terms."

## Grounding
Real interviewee — one of the 8 supply-side conversations behind Step 3's profile.

## Secondary persona (demand side, optional per this step but drafted for completeness)
**Big Ray Delgado**, owner of Copperhead Roofing & Restoration, Fort Worth — an existing direct
Osei Aerial client of 3 years. Day-in-the-life centers on the moment a hailstorm alert comes in:
Ray's phone starts ringing with homeowners wanting an inspection before their insurance claim
window narrows, and today his bottleneck is literally "how fast can Derek personally get to each
roof." Goal: document damage fast enough that his crews are the ones filing the claim, not a
competitor's. Grounding: real interviewee, one of the 6 demand-side conversations.
