# Step 1: Market Segmentation

## Starting point
Market pull: Derek has run a real, revenue-generating drone-inspection business (Osei Aerial) for
eight years and is pivoting it into a marketplace specifically because he's hit a scaling wall
(he's one pilot; storm surges create demand he personally can't fill while other qualified pilots
sit idle). Segmentation here is done on both sides separately per marketplace convention — a
supply-side brainstorm and a demand-side brainstorm, not one blended list.

## Candidate segments
| # | Segment name | End user | Distinct need | Why it's heterogeneous from other segments | Source | Side (marketplace only: supply/demand) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full-time freelance drone operators (real estate/media) diversifying into inspection work | Independent Part 107 pilot, existing small business | Wants to fill schedule gaps between real-estate/media shoots with higher-value inspection gigs | Already has commercial insurance and business infrastructure; treats this as revenue diversification, not hobby income | founder-identified | supply |
| 2 | Small drone-service companies (2-5 pilots) with spare capacity | Owner-operator of a 2-5 person drone shop | Wants overflow work to smooth utilization across their own small team | Has multiple pilots and existing dispatch/scheduling habits unlike a solo freelancer | founder-identified | supply |

| 4 | Retired ag-survey/mapping pilots with existing equipment | Retired professional pilot from an adjacent drone-use industry | Wants part-time, flexible paid flying without building a new client base from scratch | Different skill background (mapping/survey, not photography) and different equipment profile | founder-identified | supply |

13 candidate segments (6 supply-side, 7 demand-side) — above the 10+ target, generated by
brainstorming each side independently before any pairing was considered, per the marketplace
branching guidance.

## Segments carried forward to Step 2
- **Supply — Segment 1 (full-time freelance operators) + Segment 2 (small drone-service companies):**
  carried forward together as the realistic beachhead supply pool — matches Derek's actual
  existing network of ~12 known pilots, most of whom fit one of these two profiles.
- **Supply — Segment 4 (retired ag/survey pilots):** carried as a secondary supply pool worth
  scoring, since at least one real named contact (Tom Whitfield) fits it.
- **Demand — Segment 7 (storm-damage roofing contractors):** carried forward as the primary
  demand-side beachhead candidate — highest urgency, and the only segment where Derek has *any*
  existing real relationships (his 6 current Osei Aerial clients).
- **Demand — Segment 8 (independent public adjusters):** carried forward as a runner-up — smaller
  average order size but a genuinely distinct urgent-need buyer worth scoring against Segment 7.
- **Demand — Segment 10 (property management):** carried forward as a candidate follow-on
  segment (feeds Step 14) precisely because its non-storm-driven seasonality could smooth
  SkyClaim's revenue against Segment 7's spiky demand — flagged for Step 2 to weigh explicitly.

## Assumptions flagged
- Segment 9 (carrier CAT teams) and Segment 12 (general restoration contractors) are candidate —
  needs validation; Derek has no direct relationships in either and their buying process
  (procurement-heavy for #9, scope ambiguity for #12) is not yet understood.
