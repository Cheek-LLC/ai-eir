# Step 12: Decision-Making Unit (DMU)

Per this step's own template instruction: one full table per side for a marketplace.

## Supply-side DMU
| Role | Who fills it (realistic title) | Same as End User? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| End User | The pilot themselves (e.g., Marcus Webb) | — | |
| Champion | Same as End User | Yes | No separate internal champion needed — an individual decides for themselves |
| Primary Economic Buyer | Same as End User | Yes | The pilot personally accepts or declines each job; no budget approval chain |
| Influencer/Recommender | A peer pilot in the same Facebook group/meetup (per Marcus's own account of how he joined) | No | Peer reference carries real weight in this community per Step 5/11 |
| Veto-holder | A business partner, in 2-3 person shops only (e.g., Carlos Districh's shop) | No | Minor exception, not the norm for solo pilots |
| Procurement | N/A | — | Not applicable — individuals, not organizations, are deciding |

**Typical deal size & complexity (supply side):** 1 person, occasionally 2 in a small shop. This is
a collapsed, simple DMU — consistent with Step 3's finding that the pilot is (almost always) their
own economic buyer.

## Demand-side DMU
| Role | Who fills it (realistic title) | Same as End User? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| End User | Field/ops manager who books jobs day to day | — | |
| Champion | Usually the ops manager; sometimes the owner directly at smaller (10-20 crew) shops | Sometimes | Splits by company size |
| Primary Economic Buyer | Owner/GM — approves new vendor relationships even for a per-job marketplace model, per Step 3's finding that end user ≠ economic buyer here | No | Confirmed across all 3 contacted demand-side prospects in Step 9 |
| Influencer/Recommender | Peer contractors in the Roofing Contractors Association of Texas chapter | No | Same trade-association word-of-mouth channel that made this beachhead reachable (Step 2) |
| Veto-holder | **The insurance carrier/adjuster who must accept the report as valid claim documentation** | No | Not a person inside the contractor's own org — an external, indirect veto-holder. If a contractor's carrier relationships won't accept SkyClaim-formatted reports, the contractor won't adopt regardless of price or speed (flagged first in Step 6) |
| Procurement | Informal at this company size (10-50 crews) — no formal procurement department observed in any of the 3 contacted prospects | No | Formal procurement would only plausibly appear at the larger end of the beachhead range or in a Pin 2/3 follow-on segment like carrier CAT teams |

**Typical deal size & complexity (demand side):** 2-3 distinct roles typical (ops manager +
owner + the external carrier veto), more complex than supply side. This asymmetry — simple
1-person supply-side DMU vs. multi-role demand-side DMU — is itself a real structural finding: the
two sides of this marketplace have fundamentally different sales/acquisition shapes, which Step 13
must map as genuinely separate processes, not a shared one.

## Veto/blocker risks
The external-carrier veto-holder on the demand side is the single highest-leverage risk on this
entire page — it sits outside SkyClaim's and the contractor's direct control, and no amount of
product polish overcomes a carrier that rejects the report format. This should be tested directly
and early (see Step 7's flagged assumption `ka-007-report-format`).

## Implications for the acquisition approach
Teed up for Step 13: demand-side acquisition must include a step addressing carrier-acceptance
confidence (a reference/case-study argument, most likely) that has no supply-side analog; supply-
side acquisition is comparatively simple and fast (a single-person yes/no decision) but needs
sustained job-volume proof to retain, per Step 6's renewal-stage drop-off risk.
