# Step 12: Decision-Making Unit (DMU)

## DMU roles
| Role | Who fills it (realistic title) | Same as End User? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| End User | General Manager / Shift Manager (e.g., Jordan Vasquez) | No | Uses the product daily, has zero budget authority |
| Champion | Sometimes the End User GM if she hears about it and pushes up; more often a forward-leaning Director of Operations who's personally frustrated by coverage complaints reaching her | Sometimes | Champion role is genuinely ambiguous at this beachhead size — flagged as an open question, not resolved |
| Primary Economic Buyer | Director of Operations (multi-unit groups); Owner/Operator directly (smaller/family-owned groups like Kowalski Family Restaurants, Step 9) | No | Controls the budget line this would come out of (ops/labor-tech spend) |

| Procurement (if applicable) | None formally at this unit-count band — Directors of Operations at 10-50 unit groups typically approve their own tech spend without a separate procurement function | N/A | Different from what a 200+ unit enterprise group would require |

## Veto/blocker risks
The franchisor-level IT/brand-standards veto is the single biggest unknown in this table — Maria
has not yet confirmed, for any of the Step 9 prospect brands, whether their franchise agreement
restricts third-party tools touching the roster/scheduling data. This could kill a deal after the
Director of Ops has already said yes.

**Update (revision cycle, week of 2026-08-25 — addresses council required revision
[DMU-COMPLEXITY]):** Maria called 3 of the 7 Step 9 prospects directly and asked this question
outright. Results: **2 of 3 confirmed no franchisor veto applies** — Copperline Burgers (Alex
Torres) and Nair Hospitality Group (Priya Nair) are both structured so the
Director of Operations/Owner-Operator can approve back-office tools without central franchisor IT
sign-off. **1 of 3 (RiverBend QSR Group, Derek Osei) has a real, non-fatal added step**: their
franchisor requires a data-handling/security attestation review for any third-party tool touching
employee contact data, typically a 2-3 week turnaround — not a veto, but a real time cost Stage 4
of Step 13 needs to reflect. **This is a partial confirmation only** — 3 of 7 named prospects, not
all 7, and not the beachhead segment as a whole. It moves the risk from "entirely unconfirmed" to
"absent for 2 of 3 sampled, present-but-survivable for 1 of 3" — the remaining 4 Step 9 prospects
and the wider beachhead are still unconfirmed and this finding should not be over-generalized.

## Typical deal size & complexity
Roughly 2-3 distinct people typically involved (End User informs but doesn't decide; Director of
Ops decides; occasionally a franchisor IT veto) — simpler than an enterprise DMU, more than a pure
self-serve consumer sale.

## Implications for the acquisition approach
Sales approach should target the Director of Ops directly (not GMs) for the initial pitch, but
use GM pain points (Step 6/8) as the actual proof material — teed up for Step 13, not solved here.
