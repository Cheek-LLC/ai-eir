# Step 2: Select a Beachhead Market

## Candidates carried from Step 1
1. Multi-unit QSR franchise groups (10-50 units)
2. Multi-location casual-dining chains
3. Multi-location convenience-store retail
4. Home health aide agencies
5. School districts (substitute teacher coverage) — runner-up for contrast

Scoring: 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) per criterion, summed. "Reach" and "Right to win" for QSR score 5
because Maria has 10 years operating in exactly this segment and an existing peer network of ops
directors at comparable franchise groups. Convenience-store retail and home-health both score low
on "Right to win" — Maria has no direct network or credibility there yet, which is a real red flag
worth naming even though sizing looks plausible on paper.

## Selected beachhead market
**Multi-unit QSR franchise groups operating 10-50 units, in a single U.S. metro or regional
cluster.** Rationale: highest score by a wide margin, driven specifically by reach and right-to-
win (Maria's own network gives direct, warm access to Directors of Operations at comparable
groups) and word-of-mouth potential (multi-unit operators in the same brand or region reference
each other constantly through franchise-association channels and private Facebook/Slack groups).
The 10-50 unit band is deliberately chosen over larger enterprise groups (200+ units, which have
existing custom tooling and long procurement cycles) and over true single-location independents
(which can't support a cross-location backup pool at all — see Step 1, segment 13).

## Runner-ups and why they were not selected
- **Casual-dining chains** — structurally similar and a plausible fast-follow, but tipped-staff
  compensation changes the backup-worker incentive model in a way Maria hasn't tested; deferred
  rather than rejected, and named explicitly as the primary bowling-pin candidate below.
- **School districts (substitute teachers)** — sizing looks attractive on paper but competitive
  intensity is a 1: SubFinder and Frontline Education are deeply entrenched, multi-year district
  procurement cycles are the opposite of the sizing-fit worked for a fast-follow beachhead, and
  Maria has zero existing relationships in K-12 administration. Score reflects this honestly
  rather than being inflated by market size alone.

## Bowling-pin path
QSR franchise groups → casual-dining chains (same "shift manager fills an hourly call-out" shape,
adjacent buyer persona) → multi-location convenience retail (adjacent 24-hour urgency case). Home
health aide agencies and school districts are explicitly *not* on the near-term bowling-pin path;
they would require a different qualification/credentialing feature set and different buyer
relationships Maria does not currently have.
