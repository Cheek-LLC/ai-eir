# Step 11: Competitive Position Chart

Business-type branching used: **Consumer app** — axes centered on engagement/stickiness vs.
simplicity, and "alternatives" deliberately includes the status quo (solo Instagram posting) as a
plotted point, not just named app competitors, per this branch's explicit instruction.

## Chosen axes

**X-axis: Accountability structure** (none/solo → structured small-group). **Y-axis: Creative
breadth** (single-discipline tool → multi-discipline habit platform). Chosen from Steps 5/8
directly: Maya's stated pain is isolation in the habit (accountability axis), and the persona's
"day in the life" repeatedly referenced wanting this to feel different from a generic tool
(breadth/differentiation axis) — not price or feature count, which the founder initially proposed
and was pushed back on for not tracing to actual persona research.

## Alternatives considered

Status quo (posting solo to Instagram / doing nothing), Procreate (drawing tool, no habit/social
layer), Duolingo (habit/streak mechanics, wrong content domain), the Streaks app (generic habit
tracker, no creative specificity or social layer), r/SketchDaily itself (a real community, but an
unstructured one — no built-in accountability mechanic, just a shared feed).

## Position chart

## Your differentiated position

Kindling doesn't win trivially on both axes against every competitor — Procreate genuinely beats
it on creative breadth-within-a-discipline (it's a professional drawing tool; Kindling isn't
trying to be), and Duolingo genuinely beats it on proven habit-mechanic scale and polish. The
honest differentiated claim is narrower: **no current competitor combines creative-discipline
specificity with structured small-group accountability** — that gap is real, per the axes chosen
directly from persona research, but it is not a "wins everywhere" position, and this file says so
plainly rather than drawing a strawman chart.
