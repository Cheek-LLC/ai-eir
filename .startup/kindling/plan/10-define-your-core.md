# Step 10: Define Your Core

Business-type branching used: **Consumer app** — the branch's warning is direct and correctly
applied here: "'Clean design' or 'good UX' is not a Core — press for what's underneath the
design." Also directly useful: the branch names network/social effects and a
proprietary-personalization-engine-that-improves-with-data as the two consumer-app-typical real
Cores — both were tested against the three-criteria bar below and both **failed**, honestly, at
this stage.

## Candidate advantages considered

1. **The accountability-circle mechanic itself.** Rejected as a Core: any well-resourced competitor
   (Duolingo, a Streaks-app clone, a new entrant) could copy "small group sees your streak" in a
   sprint. It's a real, validated-by-the-pilot *feature*, not a structural asset.
2. **Founder's personal community credibility** (the two meetup groups, the 2-year Discord
   standing). This is real and currently doing real work (it's why the pilot happened at all, and
   why 5 of Step 9's prospects are already in conversation) — but it is Priya's personal asset, not
   the company's, and it doesn't get harder to replicate as the company grows; if anything it's the
   opposite of "grows stronger over time" as defined by this step, since a competitor with a
   *different* founder's community credibility could win a *different* community just as easily.
3. **A future pod-matching/personalization algorithm that improves with accumulated usage data**
   (the branch's second named candidate). This does not exist yet — Step 22's MVBP matches
   circles manually, by hand. There is no accumulated data to build this on top of yet.
4. **Network/social effects within a circle** (the branch's first named candidate). Partially real
   in principle (a circle gets more valuable to its members as more of *their* friends join), but
   this is a **within-circle** effect, not a cross-user platform network effect the way a
   marketplace or a social network has — a circle of 6 friends doesn't get more valuable because
   *other, unrelated* circles exist on the platform. This is a real but structurally weak version
   of the network-effects pattern this branch names, and calling it a true Core would overstate it.

## The Core

**No durable Core has been identified yet.** None of the four candidates above passes all three
criteria:

- Unique: the mechanic itself (#1) is not unique — it's copyable in a sprint by any funded
  competitor.
- Important: yes for #1 and #2 (the pilot shows the mechanic drives real behavior change, and the
  founder's community access is why the pilot happened at all) — but importance alone doesn't make
  something a Core.
- Grows: only candidates #3 and #4 could plausibly grow stronger over time, and neither exists in a
  real, accumulated-value form yet — #3 has no data to compound, and #4's within-circle effect
  doesn't compound *across* the user base the way a true network effect does.

## Resulting competitive advantages

Even without a durable Core, real near-term competitive advantages exist and should be named
honestly as advantages, not conflated with a Core: founder-market fit and community credibility in
the beachhead (feeds Step 9's real access), a validated (if small-sample) behavioral result (Step
8's 3x streak-length delta), and execution speed as a solo/small-team operator who can ship and
iterate faster than an incumbent habit-app player would bother to for this niche.

## Core or just a Moat?

**Neither, honestly — this is a "not yet identified" finding, not even a temporary moat.** The
closest thing to a moat is founder speed-to-market plus personal community access, which is real
and useful for the next 12-18 months but explicitly temporary and non-durable per this step's own
definition. This is a legitimate finding for an `idea_only`-stage company three months from its
first informal pilot, not a red flag to paper over — see this step's own "when the founder doesn't
know" guidance, which calls this exact honesty "a genuine and important finding."
